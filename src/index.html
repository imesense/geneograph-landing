Organize

Give every part of your research a proper home.

Scans in a folder, family photographs elsewhere, research questions in scattered notes — it’s easy to lose track of what you already found. GeneoGraph gives each kind of material its own place: files and sources in Archive, photos in Albums and working thoughts in Notes, all within the same project.

01 / Archive

The record has a place.

The Meowbridge marriage certificate lives with the project’s family documents, where it can be found again alongside its linked people and research.

02 / Albums

The photograph keeps its context.

The Silver and Luna photograph sits in Albums with its date, Meowbridge location and connections to the people in it.

03 / Notes

The question stays with the evidence.

Daisy’s note asks who her father was, records Barnaby Whiskerfield as a possible lead rather than a conclusion, and keeps observations, linked material and next steps together.

Connect

The research stays with the person.

Archive, Albums and Notes give different materials their own homes. Link them to the person, and you can find the family photograph and working notes directly from the family tree record while each item remains in its proper place.

Connections work both ways: a file can also identify the people or events it relates to, so evidence does not lose its family context.

Visualize

When a fixed tree isn’t enough, arrange the family your way.

Start with people already in your project, then arrange them freely with photographs, places and research notes. Shape a board that explains a complicated branch, adds context to a family story, or presents the family in a way a standard tree cannot.

Create a visual that fits the story.

Move and group people from your project, then place photographs, text and research context where they help explain the family. The board gives you room to compose a view that a fixed tree layout cannot.

Keep open questions visible.

Add a possible person or relationship with its supporting note on the board, without treating it as a settled Family Tree conclusion.
